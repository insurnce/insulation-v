What we changed after feedback

Our teacher tried several cases on the AI version of the site and gave four comments. This page shows each comment and what we did about it.

Comment 1

Add an optional button that explains clearly why a risk cannot be insured.

Done
  • Explain the decision step by step on every result. It sets out what would be insured, then each test as question → rule → why the rule exists → your request → result, then the decision, what would go wrong if we covered it anyway, and what we can cover instead.
  • A fifth test, within our scope, so life, funeral and medical requests are declined for the right reason.
  • The decision line and the summary are generated from the five test results, so they can never contradict them.
Comment 2

Some risks cannot be calculated from our own data (e.g. a lost wallet got a 1% chance). Make the AI explain its reasoning better.

Done
  • No data, no price. Every probability now comes from published data: Hong Kong Police, C&SD, the Hong Kong Observatory and SITA. The AI may only choose a row from our data table; our code does every calculation, so the steps always add up.
  • If no data measures the event, the site says "We can't price this risk yet", names the data it would need, and suggests something we can price. A lost wallet gets this answer; theft of a wallet is priced from Police data (0.15% a year).
  • A data-row check stops the AI stretching data to fit (theft data cannot price a lost item; baggage data cannot price a flight delay).
  • How did we get this chance? shows the data, the rate and the conversion to your policy period on every quote.
Comment 3

The site is most useful for filtering out risks that cannot be insured (e.g. funeral: outside micro-risk).

Kept and strengthened
  • Common test cases (lost items, flight delays, phone screens, funeral and medical, gambling, exams, losses that already happened) have fixed, hand-checked answers with full reasoning.
  • Phone-screen cover is now declined from data: 31% × 67% = 20.8% of owners a year, above our 20% limit.
Comment 4

The simulation has little feel. Add a short line on the distribution and parameters, and use a JavaScript chart library such as Plotly.

Done
  • Run the simulation button on every quote and policy, with a one-line model (e.g. storm nights ~ Poisson(λ = 1.81); other cancellations ~ Binomial(175, 1%)) and every parameter with its source.
  • Charts drawn with Plotly in the browser. Concert Trip Cover gives the same results as our R model: 6.4% losing years and HK$234,500 capital for a 1-in-200 year.
  • Roll the dice once, 100 or 1,000 times, and watch premiums and payouts add up.
  • Sliders for the other-cause rate, the number of concerts or policies, and "what if the real chance is higher".
  • Reality check: what each product would have paid in every real year of its data, including the 47 named storm evenings from 2000 to 2025.
  • Seasonal pricing for weather covers from HKO monthly records, and a live HKO check that pauses weather sales while a warning is in force.

Honest limitations

  • The 1% other-cause rate for concerts is rounded up from 0.77% documented in news reports, which miss smaller cases. Our R sensitivity test shows HK$25 is safe up to 1% and loses money in 28% of years at 2%.
  • HKO records storms, not which concerts were on. The reality check uses the average number of concerts an evening (175 ÷ 365), with a stress view of 2 concerts on every storm evening.
  • Weather covers are lumpy: in some real years a typhoon cluster would have cost more than a year's premiums. That is why we would need capital and reinsurance.
  • Police figures only count reported cases. Our theft cover pays only against a police report, so the reported rate is the one that matters.
  • The phone-screen figure is US data; it is the only large published survey we found.
  • Pool sizes (175 concerts × 100 tickets; 1,000 policies) are sales assumptions, not data.
  • The AI underwriter can word one test awkwardly on unusual requests. Prices and verdicts are always decided by our code and data.
